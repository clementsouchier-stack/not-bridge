Facts, sources and practical guidance.

Act in Synch documents legal and industry developments around generative AI in music, with a focus on clear sourcing, responsible practice and commercial decision-making.

Why this matters

The risk does not belong to one medium. It follows the music.

When generative-AI music enters a commercial production without sufficiently established provenance and rights, the uncertainty can travel with that music into every use of the finished content — advertising, film, television, games, online video, social platforms and beyond.

01

Uncertainty enters the production.

Training-data provenance, output rights, transferability or contractual protection may be incomplete or unclear.

02

The music is embedded in finished content.

Once synchronised into a campaign, film, episode, game or branded asset, replacing it can affect the whole production and its distribution chain.

03

Exposure continues after release.

The content can keep running, circulating and generating value long after the original production decision was made.

04

Detection or a rights claim can come later.

Improved detection, provenance tools, litigation or new evidence can surface questions months or years after release. A late discovery does not retroactively make earlier exploitation safe.

How the risk arises

AI-generated does not automatically mean rights-cleared.

Commercial productions depend on a documented chain of title. Uncertainty around training data, model terms or output rights can move into the finished campaign, film, series or game.

01

Training data

Protected works may be present in model training data.

02

Generative model

The legal basis for training differs by provider and jurisdiction.

03

Generated asset

Ownership, provenance and similarity may require verification.

04

Commercial production

A later rights claim can affect already-distributed content.

The practical rule

Undetected is not cleared.

A provider may allow commercial use while important questions about provenance, underlying rights, transferability or liability remain unresolved. And a track that is not identified as problematic today may be reassessed later as detection, watermarking and provenance systems improve.

Risk over time

A later detection can still reach an earlier release.

There is no “not seen, therefore safe” principle. Content may be reassessed after release as detection and provenance capabilities improve. If an infringement is ultimately established, remedies and financial exposure depend on the applicable law and facts — and continued exploitation can increase the scale of prejudice, remediation and legal cost.

What can follow if infringement is established

Stop

Courts may issue injunctions against continued infringement.

Remove

EU enforcement rules provide for measures including recall or removal from channels of commerce in appropriate cases.

Compensate

Damages can take account of actual prejudice, lost profits, unfair profits or a royalty-based benchmark, depending on the case.

Pay the process

Reasonable and proportionate legal costs may also be recoverable by the successful party.

The duration of exploitation does not mechanically determine damages. Exposure depends on the applicable law, the facts, knowledge, prejudice and remedies ordered. The point is simpler: continued exploitation does not neutralise an unresolved rights risk.

What responsible use looks like

AI is not the problem. Unclear rights are.

Responsible creative AI can exist when provenance, permissions, contracts and creator interests are documented and understood before commercial use.

01

Responsible AI

Licensed or authorised sources, transparent provenance, clear commercial terms and identifiable responsibility.

02

Unclear AI

Unknown training sources, weak warranties, uncertain chain of title and risk transferred downstream.

03

Human-created music

Identifiable creators and rights holders, established licensing pathways and a documentable chain of title.

Detection & provenance

Can AI-generated music be identified?

Sometimes — but different tools answer different questions. Signal detection, embedded watermarking and cryptographic provenance should not be treated as interchangeable, and none of them is a legal verdict.

02
