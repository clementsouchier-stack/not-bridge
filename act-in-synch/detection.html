DETECTION & PROVENANCE

Can AI-generated music be identified?

Sometimes — increasingly well, but not perfectly. Detection, watermarking and provenance answer different questions and should never be confused with a legal verdict.

Three different ways of knowing

Detection analyses. Watermarking marks. Provenance documents.

These technologies can strengthen transparency around AI-generated audio, but they operate differently and have different evidential value.

01

Signal detection

Analyses characteristics of the audio itself to estimate whether it may have been generated by a known AI system.

02

Watermarking

Embeds an identifiable signal at generation time. A detected watermark can support provenance; its absence does not prove human authorship.

03

Content provenance

Cryptographically records where media came from and how it has been modified, rather than attempting to infer origin from the sound alone.

THE CORE RULE

Detection is a signal. Not a verdict — and it can arrive after release.

An AI-detection score cannot, by itself, establish infringement, ownership, lawful or unlawful training, or whether a production has the rights required for commercial exploitation. But improving detection and provenance can surface questions about content months or years after it entered circulation. That is why absence of a signal at release should never be treated as rights clearance.

Watermarking

Know because the generator marked the output.

A watermark is fundamentally different from a detector trying to infer origin after the fact.

Google SynthID

Google DeepMind says SynthID embeds an imperceptible watermark into audio generated or published through Lyria. The watermark is designed to remain detectable through common transformations such as MP3 compression, added noise or speed changes.

Google DeepMind ↗

Important limitation

A positive SynthID result can indicate Google AI provenance. A negative result does not establish that the music was made by a human or that another generative model was not used.

Provenance

Know because the history travels with the file.

C2PA Content Credentials provide an open standard for recording and validating the origin and history of digital media, including audio. For generative AI, credentials can identify output as trained-algorithmic media and can carry information about models, inputs and processing history.

C2PA / Content Credentials

This approach does not try to guess whether an audio file “sounds like AI”. It provides signed provenance information that can be verified when credentials are present and intact.

Training-data transparency

Detection is not the same as knowing how a model was trained.

A track may be identifiable as AI-generated without telling you whether the training data was licensed. Conversely, a model can document a consent-based training approach without that proving anything about a specific output’s exclusivity or clearance.

Founded by Ed Newton-Rex, Fairly Trained certifies qualifying generative-AI models whose certified training datasets meet defined licensing, consent, open-licence, public-domain or ownership criteria.

WHAT IT CAN TELL YOU

Training-data practice

A certification can be relevant evidence about a model developer’s data-sourcing approach.

WHAT IT CANNOT TELL YOU

Output clearance

It does not by itself establish output ownership, exclusivity, client transfer, media rights or provider indemnity.

What detection cannot establish

Five questions remain legal or contractual.

Is the output infringing?

A detector cannot determine infringement by itself.

Who owns it?

Ownership depends on law, contracts and the circumstances of creation.

Was training lawful?

Detection of AI generation does not reveal the legal basis for training.

Can rights pass to the client?

That depends on the provider terms and the production contract.

Who carries the claim?

Indemnity and liability allocation are contractual questions.
